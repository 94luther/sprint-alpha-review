The merchant till where delivery apps actually lose orders

Nobody loses an order in the app. They lose it at the counter, where the order arrives on a phone nobody is holding and a rider turns up twenty minutes later to a shop that has picked nothing. The promise is two minutes to accept. What makes that mean something is the line below: a shop that has not accepted and a till that is switched off look identical on every other delivery dashboard, and they are two different phone calls to two different people.

Liquor, by the merchant's own licence the app does not pretend to know the law

Botswana's Trade Act repealed the old liquor statute and the one that replaced it is not on this machine. A trading hour invented by a delivery company is an offence waiting to happen. So the shop's own licence is the rule, and the app enforces exactly what it says. The time that matters is when it arrives, not when it was ordered.

A rider with no signal most of this country has none

A rider does not stop working because a bar disappeared. They still hand the parcel over, still take the cash, still photograph the door. Turn the signal off, do a few deliveries, then turn it back on. Nothing is ever thrown away, not even when the office refuses it.

The bank's monthly invoice where the money already is

A bank does not pay per delivery at a gate. It orders against an account, splits the cost across its own departments, and pays one invoice a month. An order with no department on it stops the invoice rather than hiding inside it.

What is not here

This is the engine, not the product. There is no camera, no rider phone, no live map and no payment provider connected. WhatsApp ordering needs a business account that nobody at Sprint owns yet. The waybill a branch would scan does not exist. Those are named on the build wall with a person against each one, rather than quietly left out of a demo.
